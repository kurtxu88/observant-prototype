User identity & data sharing

Observant asks for the minimum it needs to hold a continuous one-on-one line with each of your users — and never for raw PII it doesn't need. You stay in control: you send the invite, your users opt in, and they can opt out at any time.

What each surface needs from you

Three of the four surfaces need no identity work at all — the person is already identified on the channel where the conversation happens.

SurfaceWhat we need from youIdentity work
EmailA way to reach opted-in users — an address list, or you forward our inviteNone
SlackThe Observant app in your shared customer SlackNone — the Slack handle is the identity
DiscordThe Observant bot in your community DiscordNone — the Discord handle is the identity
In-productA stable identifier passed when our snippet loadsThe hashed Observant ID (below)

That's why email, Slack, and Discord are the fastest way to launch — you can be live this week with no SDK and no integration. In-product is the only surface that needs an identity mechanism, and it's a one-time setup.

The Observant ID — in-product identity

When Observant lives inside your product, it can't ask each user "who are you?" again — and it shouldn't hold your real user IDs. So you pass a stable, unique, non-reversible identifier when the agent loads. We call it the Observant ID.

Three properties make it work:

  • Unique — two users never share an ID.
  • Static — once assigned, it doesn't change, so each person's conversation memory persists across sessions.
  • Mappable on your side, never reversible on ours — run your internal user ID through a hash or anonymizing function. You keep the mapping; we never see the real ID.
// when the Observant agent loads in your product: Observant.setUserIdentifier(hash(yourInternalUserId)) // └─ stable, unique, one-way. // We store this; we can't reverse it.
What this costs you: a little integration work — generate the hashed ID and keep the real-ID ↔ hashed-ID mapping on your side. That one-time setup buys the richest surface: catching people at the exact moment of use.

What we deliberately never ask for

No names, no emails, no PII tied to the hashed ID — unless a user chooses to share something in their own 1:1. The hashed ID plus the conversation is enough to learn from, and your real user data stays where it belongs: with you.
